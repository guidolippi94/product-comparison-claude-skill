# Comparison modes

Identify the case from the user's input, then apply the variant. The general flow (intake, research, sheet) stays the same.

## A. Specific products (names or links)

Typical input: "compare Dyson V15 and Samsung Bespoke Jet", or two or three URLs.

- If there is a link, open the page and extract brand, model, product code and year. Many products have variants with the same name but different hardware (per market or per year): compare the variant sold in the user's market.
- If a name is ambiguous, resolve it with a search; ask the user only if two plausible candidates remain.
- Compare only what is comparable. If one product is in a very different tier (half the price, previous generation), say so in the summary.
- 2 to 6 products. Beyond that, propose a reasoned shortlist and compare the finalists.

## B. Brands

Typical input: "Bosch vs Miele vs Siemens for a washing machine".

- For each brand pick the model that best fits the needs from intake, and state which and why. The comparison is between brands represented by an equivalent model for tier and price.
- Add brand-level criteria: historical reliability (failure rates from independent surveys), service and spare parts network in the user's market, warranty length, repair and update policy.
- If a brand has several very different lines, state the line compared; never generalise one line's data to the whole brand.

## C. Generic category

Typical input: "best robot vacuum under 400 for a home with pets".

- Build a shortlist of 3-5 candidates first: start from the most recent independent comparative tests (last 12-18 months), cross at least two sources and keep models that appear consistently. Add an outsider only with solid evidence.
- In the sheet, explain in one line how the shortlist was made and what was excluded and why.
- Check that candidates are still on sale in the user's market and within budget.

## D. One product plus alternatives

Typical input: "I'm looking at this one, are there better alternatives?" with a link.

- The user's product is the reference (first column). Find 2-4 alternatives that beat it on at least one stated priority or cost less at similar quality.
- If the reference is already the best choice, say so clearly: it is a valid result.

## How many products, how deep

| Situation | Products | Criteria | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Simple purchase (< 100 in local currency units of a mid-income market) | 2-4 | 4-5 | Lighter research, fewer sources per product |
| Medium purchase | 3-5 | 5-6 | Standard case |
| Major purchase or health and safety | 3-5 | 6-7 | At least two independent tests per product, studies where they exist |

Scale the price bands to the user's market and currency.
